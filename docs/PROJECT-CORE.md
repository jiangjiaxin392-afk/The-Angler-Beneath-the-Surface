# The Angler: canonical project core

This note distils the conceptual centre of Aaron Jiang's 2026 Final Project proposal. It contains no student ID or private contact information. The proposal remains the primary source; the current README remains the authority for final implementation details.

## Central question

> How does human agency operate when a probabilistic system can be steered, but its exact output—and even the target answer—cannot be fully specified in advance?

## Core proposition

*The Angler: Beneath the Surface?* uses lure fishing as a procedural metaphor for generative AI. An angler can define a target species and manipulate location, lure, retrieve and timing, but cannot select one exact fish in advance. Likewise, an AI user can choose a model, structure a prompt and supply context, yet these interventions constrain a field of possible outputs rather than determine a single response.

The work therefore treats prompting as probabilistic navigation rather than direct retrieval. Each response is a sample from a constrained but indeterminate possibility space. Repetition is not simply retrying after failure: comparison between outputs allows the user to recalibrate the prompt and to revise what they recognise as the target.

Human judgement is constitutive rather than merely corrective. The participant does not only verify whether an output is good; they decide whether it corresponds to a target that may itself become clearer through the process. Meaning emerges through selection, comparison and recognition, not through generation alone.

## What the fishing metaphor means

- The target fish represents an approximate information need rather than one fully specified answer.
- Model choice, tackle and prompt structure constrain or redistribute the probability of possible outcomes.
- Water, weather and hidden fish behaviour represent conditions that remain only partly controllable or knowable.
- Each cast represents sampling from the response space.
- Repeated catches allow comparison and iterative calibration of both method and target.
- Keep or release represents the user's recognition that a response does or does not correspond to the evolving target.

The work does **not** claim that fish and AI are equivalent. A fish is a living organism; an AI model is a computational system shaped by data, architecture, training and deployment. Their relationship in the artwork is procedural, not literal.

## Intended audience experience

The main loop is preparation, casting, sampling, comparison and recalibration. The participant can influence the distribution of outcomes without selecting one exact result. Repetition should reveal how the desired answer is progressively articulated through encounters with alternatives.

The intended takeaway is not merely that AI can be wrong or that its answers require checking. The deeper concern is the mismatch between interfaces of command and systems of probability: prompting feels like asking for an answer, while functionally it is a practice of constraining, sampling and recognising among possible responses.

## Writing guardrails

When describing the project publicly:

- foreground probability, constrained possibility, an approximate or evolving target, iterative calibration and human agency;
- describe the fishing structure as a procedural metaphor;
- distinguish steering a distribution from selecting an exact output;
- explain that comparison can revise both the prompt and the user's understanding of the target;
- avoid reducing the work to a fishing game, a prompt-engineering tutorial or a good-answer/bad-answer scoring system;
- avoid reducing the meaning to the familiar claim that AI answers need human checking;
- avoid implying that the system independently verifies facts;
- distinguish the conceptual proposal from current technical implementation details.

## Concise public description

*The Angler: Beneath the Surface?* explores how human intention is negotiated, rather than simply expressed, when working with generative AI. Visitors enter a question, choose conditions, cast, compare and try again. Yet prompting does not retrieve a predefined answer: each response can reshape both the question and what the user comes to recognise as desirable. The fishing metaphor makes this negotiation physical, while also exposing its limits. An angler may choose the lure, but cannot decide which fish exist in the pond. Likewise, models, training data and platform rules structure what can appear. The work asks: when we prompt AI, are we searching for an answer, or learning what to want?
